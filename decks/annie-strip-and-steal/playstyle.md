# Annie - Strip and Steal — playstyle

## Mulligan (deal 4, mulligan up to 2 — draw BEFORE recycle, 117.2 precedes 117.3)

**Keep:** any hand with a 1-2 rune removal spell (Hextech Ray, Gust) *and* a
body that costs 4 or less. Vex - Apathetic is the single best card to keep.

**Keep Bone Skewer** — it wants to be hidden early, and it is the only card that
shows you their hand, which makes every later strip a targeted one.

**Mulligan** hands with two or more 6-drops, and hands with no removal. This
deck's removal is its tempo; a hand of threats loses the battlefields by turn 3.

**Do not** keep Conscription or Blind Fury as your only action — both are 4-5
runes and neither affects the board before turn 3.

## Early game (turns 1-3)

The runes arrive fast — 2, 4, 6 by turn 3 — so this is not a slow start in
practice. Priorities in order:

1. **Hide Bone Skewer on turn 1-2 if you can spare `{any rune}`.** It costs a
   rune to hide and {0} to flip, and it is Reaction speed from face down while
   being Neutral speed from hand — you are buying a speed upgrade, not just a
   discount. Hiding it also reveals their hand *later*, at the moment you need
   the information.
2. **Land Evelynn or Insightful Investigator.** Bodies first. A battlefield you
   never contest is a battlefield you never take back.
3. **Do not tap out.** Leave 1-2 runes up from turn 2 onward. Annie readies 2 at
   end of turn, so the correct habit is: spend down to the point where Annie's
   2 covers your cheapest Reaction (Gust at 1 rune, Hextech Ray at 1).

## Mid game (turns 4-7) — the actual deck

**Vex - Apathetic is the card you are playing around, on both sides.** Once she
is at a battlefield, every unit they play there is stunned and cannot move that
turn. Stun is *not* removal — the stunned unit still blocks and still needs its
**full** Might in damage to die (423.1.c) — so Vex only converts to a lock when
you can kill the stunned body the same turn. **Sequence: let them commit the
unit, let Vex stun it, then burn it.** Holding Hextech Ray for this is worth
more than using it proactively.

**Scorchclaw is the resource turn.** `[Hunt 2]` fires on **hold**, not just
conquer, so every turn Scorchclaw stands on a battlefield you bank 2 XP. Get it
down by turn 3-4 and the XP for Insightful Investigator is never a question.
At 3+ XP it also enters ready and gets +1 Might.

**Kharox is a two-turn plan, so start it early.** Cast it (6 runes) on a turn
where you can afford to tap low, then Empower next turn for `{6}{Chaos}{Chaos}`
— 6 energy plus **recycling two Chaos runes**, which permanently drops you from
12 runes to 10. Check their trash *before* committing: if it has no unit worth
stealing, the Empower is 6 runes for Burn 3, which is nearly nothing. **Empowered
is binary (441.1.b) — each Kharox fires exactly once.**

**Blind Fury:** cast it when you have runes spare, not when you need an answer.
It banishes their top card and plays it free — you must be able to pay any Power
cost on what you flip, so keep a rune of each colour available.

## Late game (turns 8+) and closing

**The 8th point is easy for this deck, and that is unusual.** 471.1.b.1 only
restricts points gained through a **Conquer**; 471.1.a.1 exempts every other
source, and Hold is not a Conquer. So: **hold a battlefield at 7 points and you
win.** Do not go looking for a double conquest.

Two closing routes, in order of preference:
1. **Hold with Draven - Audacious.** 6 Might, Deflect, and it scores a point the
   first time it wins a combat each turn on top of the hold. Clear the blocker
   with removal *first* — if it dies in combat, **they** score a point.
2. **Power Nexus**, when presented. Pay 4 Power (four **recycled** runes, which
   is a permanent economy hit) to score 1 point off a hold. Do this only as the
   winning point or when the game is otherwise going long — it is cashing in
   your economy, not an engine.

**Tibbers is a board wipe you own, not a free one.** 3 damage — 4 with Annie -
Fiery — to **all** units at battlefields, yours included. Cast it when you are
behind on board, never when you are holding with a 3-Might Scorchclaw.

## Showdown priority

With Annie's 2 readied runes live on their turn, the ranked use is:
1. **Gust** (1 rune) on a lone unit that is about to hold a battlefield — they
   lose control at cleanup with nothing standing there (190.4.c) and score
   nothing. This is the highest-value rune you will ever spend.
2. **Hextech Ray** (1 rune) to finish a stunned body during their attack.
3. **Bone Skewer** from Hidden ({0}) — see below.
4. Hold the runes. A turn where you spend nothing on their turn is not a wasted
   turn if the alternative was a bad trade.

## Which battlefield to present, and how to play each

**Bo3 presentation order** — neutral in game 1, then match the seat:

- **Game 1: Void Gate.** Blind pick, turn-order neutral.
- **On the play (games 2-3): Power Nexus.**
- **On the draw (games 2-3): Ravenbloom Conservatory.**

*Caveat:* this assumes the loser-chooses-turn-order convention applies at your
event. Rule 115 says turn order is a fair random method and 486 adds no game-2
exception — **confirm it against the actual event document.** If it does not
apply, every game is blind and you should present Void Gate twice.

**Matchup override:** against a burn or damage-spell deck, never present Void
Gate — it doubles their removal as readily as yours. Present Ravenbloom
Conservatory in game 1 instead.

### Void Gate — *spells and abilities affecting units here each deal 1 Bonus Damage*
- With Annie - Fiery in the Champion Zone, **Hextech Ray at this battlefield is
  5 damage for one rune.** Fight here on purpose; make it the battlefield you
  contest, not the one you concede.
- The bonus applies to **abilities** too, so Kog'Maw-style deathknells and
  Tibbers both scale. Tibbers here is 5 to everything — including yours.
- **The mistake:** treating it as your battlefield by default. 715.4 means no
  damage dealt, no bonus — it does nothing for the strip half of the deck, so
  against a deck you are out-carding rather than out-removing, it is blank.

### Power Nexus — *hold → pay 4 Power to score 1 point*
- **The cost is four recycled runes, not four exhausted ones.** You drop from 12
  runes to 8 and must re-channel at 2/turn. Annie readying 2 does **not** give
  recycled runes back.
- Therefore: use it for the **winning** point, or when you are so far ahead on
  board that the economy no longer matters. Using it at 3 points to go to 4 is
  how you lose the following three turns.
- **The mistake:** paying it while a Kharox Empower is still pending. Kharox
  needs `{Chaos}{Chaos}` recycled on top; doing both leaves you at 6 runes.

### Ravenbloom Conservatory — *defend here → reveal top card, keep it if it's a spell*
- It triggers on **defending**, so present it when you expect to be under
  pressure, and then **actually defend on it** rather than retreating to base.
- 19 of 39 cards are spells, so it is close to a coin flip every combat, and a
  non-spell is recycled to the bottom rather than lost.
- **The mistake:** moving your holder off it to contest elsewhere. The card is
  paying you to stand still and get attacked, which is also what a hold deck
  wants — let them come.

## The Bone Skewer line, in order

Bone Skewer is the deck's most sequence-sensitive card.

1. **Hide it a turn early**, at a battlefield you control. Cost `{any rune}`.
2. **Flip it during their turn**, for {0}, at Reaction speed. From hand it is
   Neutral speed and cannot respond to anything.
3. It reveals their hand — **read it before choosing**, and remember it for the
   rest of the game.
4. You choose a **unit** from their hand; they play it to a battlefield **you**
   name, and it enters **stunned**.
5. **Name the battlefield where your removal can reach it.** Void Gate if it is
   live. A stunned unit still blocks and still needs full Might in damage — you
   have not removed anything until you finish it.

**The mistake that loses the card:** hiding it at a battlefield you then lose
control of. A Hidden card is lost with the battlefield. Limit is 1 Hidden card
per battlefield and there are 2 battlefields in 1v1, so you can have at most 2
face down at once.
